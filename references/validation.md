# Validation

Validation buys evidence, not polish. Design the smallest reversible action that can change the decision.

## Test ladder

1. Confirm the buyer group and recurring problem from current public evidence.
2. Create the smallest artifact needed to make the offer understandable: a sample, mock, diagnostic, demo, or one-page explanation.
3. Put one exact proposition in front of reachable buyers through an allowed channel.
4. Record behavior: reply, interview acceptance, qualified lead, preorder, payment attempt, or explicit rejection.
5. Decide `persist`, `adapt`, or `stop` against the predeclared evidence budget and stop rule.

Views, likes, generated assets, competitor prices, and model opinions are not direct demand. Record them as indirect signals. A positive direct market signal must identify what the buyer did, when it happened, and which offer it concerned.

Do not build beyond the validation sample before direct evidence. If evidence is negative or silent after the agreed budget, checkpoint the result and change the claim, channel, or direction rather than manufacturing success.
